## Q1. Open-weight model families (who could use frontier-ish AI, and on what terms)

### Takeaway
Open weights went from a leak (LLaMA, Mar 2023) to Western "open-core" families (Llama, Mistral, Gemma, Phi, gpt-oss), then to Chinese labs setting the pace in 2025–26 (DeepSeek, Qwen, Kimi, GLM, MiniMax). In 2026 the trend flipped on licensing: flagship Chinese releases (Kimi K3, GLM-5.3) moved from MIT to bespoke licences, while Meta killed open Llama (Apr 2026) and returned with Apache-2.0 Muse Glimmer (Aug 2026). Fully open (data + code + checkpoints) models remain a small niche (OLMo, Pythia, BLOOM).

## Q3. Training, fine-tuning, research substrates and data (who could build/customize models)

### Takeaway
PyTorch + Hugging Face Transformers/Hub became the universal substrate. LoRA/QLoRA + bitsandbytes + PEFT/Unsloth/Axolotl let individuals fine-tune on one GPU. ## Q5. Agents, coding harnesses and the hype-then-fade cycle

### Takeaway
The 2023 autonomous-agent wave (AutoGPT, BabyAGI, GPT-Engineer) peaked on stars and demos, then faded or pivoted. What endured were human-in-the-loop coding agents (Aider → Cline → OpenCode) and, in 2025–26, general "personal agent" harnesses (OpenClaw, Hermes Agent, DeepSeek Harness) with record-breaking star velocity.
